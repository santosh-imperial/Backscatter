## What these data can establish

We have 31 sites, distributed 7/7/17 across three provider-confirmed supplier batches. Batch membership is a legitimate target for a site-level classifier two-sample test (C2ST). It is not an acceptable/defective outcome label. A model that differentiates folders has not demonstrated manufacturing-defect detection. Physical specimen independence is unknown; patches, particles, graph nodes and image windows cannot increase material n.

Fresh, uncycled graphite–Si/SiOx is confirmed by Santosh. Pixelwise bright-phase chemistry, graphite instances, exact formulation, collector direction and process/electrochemical outcomes remain unverified. Lengths stay in pixels. Contrast stretching, bright low contrast, raised pore black level, ETD preparation texture and Inlens charging can all provide shortcuts to batch membership. The working reference contains its own acquisition and long-void subgroups; it is not an approved set of defect-free examples.

Local evidence already supports caution:

- Existing DINO novelty tracks acquisition descriptors and changes rankings with input resolution (D25; `analysis_cache/ml/novelty_correlates.csv` and `docs/qc_plan.md`). Per-image median/IQR normalisation did not establish acquisition invariance.
- The historical flag-inclusive logistic classifier obtained its strongest discrimination from ETD boundary sharpness. The effective production classifier uses the material list, fits inside the pipeline and refits during jackknife stability. Historical flag-inclusive results are an acquisition comparator, not material accuracy.
- E18 centroid spacing was sensitive to tiny bright rim fragments and component-size choices. Graph construction inherits that problem unless objects are reviewed.
- E25 local bright dispersion partly tracks loading/size; bright–void proximity is descriptive and threshold-sensitive. Exact bright/void raster adjacency is constant zero on usable sites. It is unsuitable as a contact edge or an additional variable.
- All 31 nominal pore-mode flags are unresolved. Joint metrics use the separate data-derived grey-pore flag, report fallback coverage and retain expert-mask uncertainty. Neither ordinary acquisition nor a negative acquisition-only prediction score validates a mask.

These observations come from the existing E18/D25/E25 outputs, not a new evaluation. There are currently zero independently reviewed phase masks in the annotation benchmark. No segmentation accuracy, defect sensitivity or unseen-batch performance can be reported.

## What can be adapted from the literature

Frozen encoders are plausible because DINOv2 demonstrates reusable pretrained features with frozen linear/nonparametric downstream evaluations. Its published benchmarks do not establish sensitivity to our electrode morphology or invariance to SEM acquisition. Using the cached encoder avoids fitting a high-capacity representation to these few sites. [Oquab et al., DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07193v2).

PatchCore provides a useful design pattern: a pretrained patch representation, a representative nominal memory and nearest-neighbour evidence. Our working reference is heterogeneous and lacks verified nominal/defective labels. The proposed CLS-tile audit is inspired by that pattern; it is not a reproduction of PatchCore's locally aggregated CNN features or its benchmark evaluation. [Roth et al., PatchCore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265), [authors' implementation](https://github.com/amazon-science/patchcore-inspection).

Convolution does not require training a CNN. Gabor filters use fixed oriented/frequency kernels; wavelet scattering combines fixed convolutions, modulus and averaging and can capture texture information beyond a power spectrum. Deformation stability does not establish robustness to clipping, contrast remapping or charging. A small installed Gabor bank is the feasible first test; scattering is deferred. [skimage Gabor documentation](https://scikit-image.org/docs/0.23.x/api/skimage.filters.html#skimage.filters.gabor), [Bruna and Mallat, scattering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.1513).

Supervised segmentation is a stronger long-term application of CNNs here. U-Net was demonstrated with annotated biomedical images, including neuronal electron microscopy; an original materials study used annotated steel micrographs to extract particle-size and zone-width distributions. Those results support a segmentation-to-KPI workflow, not transfer of pretrained biological/steel masks to graphite–Si/SiOx or a universal minimum image count. [Ronneberger et al., U-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597), [DeCost et al., quantitative metallography](https://www.nist.gov/publications/high-throughput-quantitative-metallography-complex-microstructures-using-deep-learning).

## Why training a GNN is deferred

Message passing can learn how node attributes and neighbourhoods jointly predict a labeled target. We do not yet have that target for battery/manufacturing outcomes or independently validated node/edge definitions. Thirty-one image graphs do not become thousands of independent training samples because they contain many particles. A folder classifier could learn acquisition-sensitive object counts, sizes and frame geometry and still provide no defect evidence.

A corrected primary microstructure study represents simulated 3-D grains as nodes with physical grain adjacency and predicts supervised magnetostriction targets. Those ingredients differ from 2-D bright-fragment proximity and unlabeled QC outcomes. Its author correction reports that the original implementation omitted message passing. This makes a practical ablation particularly relevant: compare a graph model with the same node summaries and a model with no/shuffled edges before attributing improvement to spatial learning. No published performance number is transferred here. [Dai et al., corrected article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-021-00574-w), [author correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-022-00804-9).

**At this n, a GNN's added defect-detection benefit is not identifiable.** Geometry may still reveal useful patterns, so use the visible fixed graph first. A future GNN pilot needs reviewed objects, a justified graph relationship, independent manufacturing batches with QC/process/cell outcomes, and a batch/specimen-held-out comparison against (i) existing KPIs, (ii) pooled node attributes, (iii) fixed graph summaries and (iv) the same GNN with edges removed/shuffled. Choose data volume from learning curves and outcome diversity, not a borrowed claim that a particular number of images is enough. Attribution maps explain a fitted prediction; they do not validate a battery mechanism.

Pore-region/skeleton graphs are a possible later representation after void-boundary and skeleton validation. Their 2-D connectivity and widths cannot supply 3-D transport/contact topology. Dense DINO token maps would also require new extraction: current caches contain tile CLS vectors only. Transformer feature-map artifacts have been documented; a coloured token/attention map is not a validated pixel defect mask. [Darcet et al., Vision Transformers Need Registers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16588).

## Two concrete experiment protocols

Every count, scale, graph rule and control setting below is a **proposed prespecified setting**, not an empirically validated optimum or product tolerance. Assign exact candidate IDs/keys and register definitions before extraction; no further permission step is implied.

### Protocol G: fixed bright-object graph audit

1. **Freeze before looking at new outputs.** Use current trimmed BSE masks and E25 conventions: 8-connected bright components, area ≥50 px², exclude image-clipped components. Keep exact coordinates/coverage. Nodes carry area and existing shape descriptors; optional B01 void distance/ring values are annotations, not edge definitions. Bright-dependent measurements require known-false data-derived `bright_low_contrast`; joint bright/pore annotations additionally require known-false `grey_pore`. Missing quality stays unavailable. Report unresolved pore thresholds separately.
2. **Build one fixed graph per site.** Undirected union of each centroid's 3 nearest distinct neighbours in pixel Euclidean distance; deterministic coordinate tie-breaking. No intensity, folder identity, collector assumption or learned radius. Edges mean proximity. Require at least 10 retained nodes and 10 edges for this pilot; that is a proposed coverage floor, not a statistical sufficiency claim.
3. **Keep four scalar summaries.** Median edge length (px); edge-length IQR/median; axial resultant `abs(mean(exp(2j*edge_angle)))`; log-area assortativity as Pearson correlation of endpoint log-areas over both directed copies of each undirected edge. Zero variance/no valid geometry gives NaN with a reason. Do not treat degree/connected components of a constructed kNN graph as physical continuity. Show existing bright fraction, D50 and E25 dispersion beside these values to expose redundancy.
4. **Sensitivity and controls.** Recompute with paired threshold offsets −5/0/+5, and area floor 100 px² as a prespecified sensitivity only; do not pick the setting that separates folders. Show retained/clipped/removed counts, node/edge overlays and whether the substantive reading changes. Carry the acquisition-only control below. Register exact IDs/keys and these definitions before extraction.
5. **Evaluation.** Per-site measurements, all pairwise raw-unit median differences and site-bootstrap intervals under stated provisional site independence; use quality-matched and ordinary-reference sensitivity views. A fixed four-feature L1-logistic C2ST is optional, using the established fold-local site pipeline and site-label permutations, explicitly testing batch identity. It stays separate from the primary five-KPI family and decisions. No feature selection by known AUC; any post-inspection revision needs a new development version.
6. **Go/stop.** Go to annotation review if the graph highlights a distinguishable arrangement beyond size/loading and its reading survives prespecified sensitivity. Stop a material claim for tiny-fragment/edge sensitivity or acquisition shortcuts. No expert object labels means exploratory geometry regardless of p-values; no outcome labels means no defect accuracy.

### Protocol F: balanced frozen-encoder audit

1. **Keep the existing representation.** Reuse cached DINOv2 ViT-S/14 CLS vectors for complete, non-overlapping 512-px BSE tiles and existing `n1c3` median/IQR normalisation. Input 518 is primary; 224 is a declared sensitivity. No encoder fine-tuning, added backbone or newly tuned crop scale.
2. **Equalise reference-site influence.** Eligibility requires finite cached vectors and at least 32 complete tiles. Select 32 tiles per eligible reference site using a fixed seeded coordinate-based selection independent of labels/intensity. Use exactly those balanced rows for PCA (32 dimensions, capped by matrix rank) and the k=5 reference memory. Do not let taller strips contribute more fitting or memory rows. Retain the heterogeneous reference in the all-sites descriptive view; prespecify quality-matched and ordinary-reference sensitivity views, rather than choosing a reference subset by the most favourable scores. If a new site has insufficient coverage, report that and abstain from this protocol rather than silently changing its sampling rule. Scoring all complete query tiles is allowed; tile median/P90 remain one site summary each.
3. **Refit reference-dependent steps.** Hold out every reference site completely, including its tiles, from PCA and memory in reference LOO. Any C2ST or label permutation involving embeddings must also refit preprocessing/PCA inside the fold/permutation. The pretrained encoder remains fixed because it was not trained on these labels. Preserve the current full-reference-query versus one-site-smaller-LOO distinction; show it as a method sensitivity, not calibrated equivalent-tail probabilities. A matched-reference-count calibration would be a separate specified method, not silently substituted.
4. **Explain and control.** Save the highest-ranked query tiles, nearest reference tiles, coordinates, original BSE/ETD crops, acquisition flags and existing morphology overlays. A tile heatmap localises a 512-px field, not the pixels responsible for a defect. Compare balanced with the existing unbalanced calculation as a coverage audit; do not choose by the most attractive batch ranking. Compare 518/224 and quality-matched/ordinary-reference readings.
5. **Small acquisition challenge, if time remains.** Prespecify one ordinary site per batch plus the known low-contrast and grey-pore example crops before scoring. Apply non-clipping affine remaps and gamma 0.75/1.25 to copies, retaining raw originals; recompute normalisation/features. Report score movement and nearest-neighbour changes. Saturation, lost contrast and preparation artifacts are not reversible normalisation targets. Synthetic intensity changes are controls, not material labels or generalisation validation.
6. **Go/stop.** Keep the encoder as a retrieval/review assistant if neighbours are useful to an expert and sensitivity is disclosed. Stop material interpretation if contrast/preparation explains the retrieved differences or rank conclusions vary strongly with resolution/coverage. A credible new morphology observation needs independent image review; it cannot automatically alter the verdict.

For both protocols, use the **same acquisition-only control**: fold-local ridge (alpha=1, imputation/scaling fitted on training sites) predicting each proposed site summary from bright separation, BSE p1/std/empty-bin fraction, ETD boundary sharpness and frame height, plus within-batch correlations and quality-stratified plots. Show the acquisition-only batch classifier beside any geometry C2ST. Covariates can encode material and reference subgroups; prediction/adjustment is not causal attribution. Negative held-out predictability does not demonstrate invariance. Freeze the control design, report usable n and treat the screen as exploratory.

## One-class models and uncertainty

The supplier-promised Batch 3 distribution now supplies a legitimate reference for a one-class/OOD comparator; manufacturing-outcome labels are not required for this target. Few sites, uncertain specimen grouping and measurement/acquisition sensitivity still argue for one simple prespecified comparator rather than sweeping isolation forests, Gaussian mixtures, one-class SVMs and autoencoders. Start from a compact interpretable site panel and distribution or nearest-reference comparisons; do not fit a rich covariance model to hundreds of embedding coordinates on a few sites. The one-class SVM parameter `nu` bounds training errors/support-vector fraction; it is not a QC false-alarm probability. [Official OneClassSVM documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.svm.OneClassSVM.html).

Conformal calibration requires representative reference calibration, the applicable exchangeability/independence assumptions and correct score-fitting separation. It need not be defect-free: heterogeneity can be part of the target distribution when sampling is representative. For ordinary nonrandomised split-conformal ranks, the smallest possible p-value is `1/(n_cal + 1)`. Even assigning all 17 reference sites to calibration would give a floor above 0.05; a genuine training/calibration split is coarser. This is a mathematical resolution limit, not a measured false-alarm result. Unknown specimen grouping and changes in preparation or sampling can invalidate calibration. Current novelty LOO percentiles are not conformal p-values or probability correct. [Bates et al., full-text introduction and rank construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.08279v3).

Keep measurement perturbations, sampling intervals, decision stability and reference ranks separately labeled. No score threshold becomes an accept/reject tolerance. Freeze definitions, quality gates, aggregation, weights and method versions before the unseen batch is examined. If it is used to choose an algorithm or repair a representation, it becomes development evidence; one new batch cannot establish broad supplier generalisation.
