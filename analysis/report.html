Incoming electrode microscopy: detailed exploratory audit

All 93 TIFF images · 31 field IDs · three batches · 3 October 2026

No approved baseline, defect labels, material identity, sampling design or verified calibration supplied. This report describes the images; it does not issue accept/reject verdicts or infer manufacturing performance.

Main findings to review

  • All 93 images decoded. No exact pixel duplicates. Every field has three dimension-matched detector views.
  • Batch_2 has higher BSE thresholded dark fractions on average, with wide sampling uncertainty. This is not porosity or proof of a defect.
  • Batch_3 has lower BSE gradient texture and contrast on average. Four fields have raised dark intensity levels, a possible imaging confounder.
  • Threshold ±10 sensitivity is large. Inlens endpoint-white fractions reach about 6.75%, requiring review for saturation.
  • Colored export borders are excluded with a fixed four-pixel edge crop. Calibration remains unverified.

What these measurements mean

  • Mean, spread, entropy and clipping describe image intensity and quality; detector contrast is not a chemical assay.
  • Gradient RMS measures fine-scale image variation, combining microstructure, focus, noise and detector effects. Anisotropy is horizontal divided by vertical gradient energy; it is not directly particle orientation.
  • Tile mean SD measures coarse spatial contrast heterogeneity using a 3 by 5 grid.
  • Dark fraction is the proportion below a common channel-specific threshold, pooled across all supplied images and weighted by pixel count. Dark regions can include voids, shadowing or low-signal material. This is not validated porosity.
  • Dark run lengths describe thresholded texture on thumbnails. Their units are thumbnail pixels; they are not segmented particle or pore sizes.

Full-resolution histograms provide intensity statistics and dark fractions. Texture uses aspect-preserving thumbnails with maximum dimension 1400 pixels. It is exploratory and should be recomputed on a calibrated common grid for final QC. Threshold sensitivity at ±10 gray levels is recorded in the inventory. Pooled thresholds use incoming batches, so they must be replaced by frozen baseline-derived thresholds for the unseen-batch test.

Validation and next steps

  1. Confirm baseline, labels, material identity, scale, specimen grouping and acquisition settings.
  2. Review representative fields plus intensity/texture outliers with a materials expert.
  3. Annotate a small sample of phases and particles; evaluate segmentation boundaries and threshold sensitivity.
  4. Use field/specimen-level holdouts. Avoid treating correlated pixels, patches or channels as replicates.
  5. Define practical equivalence tolerances and freeze the baseline reference before the unseen batch arrives.
