Design constraints carried over from the analysis: Batch 3 is the working reference but not a clean baseline; its sub-populations (grey-pore group, three cracked sites) must stay visible; raw intensity is never a material KPI; lengths in px with nominal µm; acquisition flags are first-class outputs.

**Two framing rules adopted after review.**
- *"Different from the reference" and "acceptable" are different questions.* A null result from a weak test is not acceptance. Until practical tolerances exist, the best outcome we can state is **"consistent with the working reference, within what this test can detect"**, with the detectable change written next to it. Any release recommendation is provisional.
- *Batch-wide drift and localized defects are separate decision paths.* A single severe delamination-like void must trigger investigation even when the batch median does not move.

## 2. Notebook sections

### 2.0 Small-sample discipline (governs everything below)
Effective sample sizes: 7 sites (Batch 1), 7 (Batch 2), 17 (Batch 3), of which 10 are "ordinary" once the grey-pore and cracked sites are set aside. Patches multiply pixels, not evidence. Rules:

1. **Primary KPI set, at most five, kept closest to validated measurements**: crack-like void fraction, largest void (2-D equivalent diameter), macro-pore area fraction, bright-phase area fraction, bright-phase 2-D area-weighted D50. Stereological estimates (volume fractions, unfolded 3-D sizes) are **secondary** until their assumptions are checked. Only the primary five carry the verdict and are multiplicity-corrected as a family. All other KPIs are secondary: reported with effect sizes and intervals, descriptive, never corrected into silence and never driving a verdict on their own. For every KPI the report states the **usable site count after quality flags** (e.g. bright-phase KPIs on Batch 1 rest on 5 reliable sites, not 7), and the usable count — not the folder count — enters every test and MDC.
2. **Site-level permutation tests** for every p-value: exact enumeration where the statistic is simple and the allocation count is manageable (7 vs 17 = 346,104 allocations is fine for a difference of medians), **Monte Carlo permutation with the number of resamples stated** for classifiers, multivariate statistics and power simulations. Every quantity that depends on which sites are "reference" — MAD scaling, standardisation, classifier preprocessing, PCA for embeddings — is **re-fitted inside each permutation and each held-out evaluation**. Effect sizes with intervals are reported ahead of p-values.
3. **Decision stability by leave-one-site-out jackknife** (seven honest re-verdicts for a 7-site batch). Bootstrap over sites is used only for interval estimates and labelled approximate.
4. **Minimum detectable change on the first screen** of every report, per primary KPI, so "consistent" is always read together with what could not have been seen.
5. **Exceeding the ordinary-reference maximum is an evidence flag, not a defect call.** Under an i.i.d. null, one incoming site exceeds the maximum of ten reference sites with probability 1/11 ≈ 9 %, and at least one of seven sites does so with probability 7/17 ≈ 41 % — for a single KPI, before other KPIs or patches. The rule therefore cannot double as a 5 % false-alarm criterion. A flag becomes a **credible localized anomaly** only when (a) the severity margin is meaningful (beyond the reference maximum by at least the ordinary-reference MAD of per-site maxima, threshold fixed in §2.1), (b) the measurement is reliable on that site (no low-contrast / grey-pore flag for the KPI concerned; stable under the ±5-level threshold band), and (c) the evidence image has been reviewed and the anomaly is visible. Flags that do not meet (a)–(c) are listed as evidence, not as verdict drivers.
6. **Patches are for within-site variability and for localizing evidence only.** They never enter n. Any patch-level model uses grouped cross-validation with sites as groups, site-level permutation nulls, and **equal influence per site** (patch weights normalised within site, or site-level aggregation of patch predictions).
7. **Honest provenance of every choice.** We have already inspected pairwise results and selected sub-groups during the dataset analysis, so no choice is independent of Batches 1–3. The configuration cell therefore carries the wording **"Developed using exploratory analysis of Batches 1–3; frozen before the unseen batch arrived"**, and the notebook asserts at run time that the configuration hash matches the frozen one. The hash establishes stability *after* the freeze, not prior independence. No model, threshold or KPI is added after the unseen result is seen; if one ever is, the unseen batch is relabelled development data and the report says so.
8. **Batch 1 vs Batch 2 is 7 vs 7**: the smallest achievable two-sided permutation p-value is ≈ 0.0006 and power is low; the report says why that comparison will mostly return "cannot distinguish".
9. **Every report carries a "limits of this dataset" paragraph**: usable sample sizes per KPI, whether sites are known to come from distinct specimens (unconfirmed — asked of the organisers), reference heterogeneity, MDC range, 2-D sections, nominal scale, unconfirmed chemistry.

### 2.1 Configuration
One cell: `REFERENCE = "Batch_3"`, `COMPARE = ["Batch_1", "Batch_2"]` (+ unseen folder when it lands), site flags (low-contrast, grey-pore, cracked) with a switch for *flag-and-show* (default) versus *exclude*, false-alarm rate α (default 0.05), target power (0.80), the primary-KPI list, consequence weights, severity-margin and decision thresholds — all under the provenance statement of §2.0 rule 7 and hashed — and the seed. Everything downstream reads this.

### 2.2 Feature extraction
Calls the shared module. Produces per-site KPI table, per-image quality flags, per-particle table, 512-px patch table (patch-level KPIs for drift maps and local-anomaly checks), and patch embeddings. Cached by content hash of the folder.

### 2.3 Reference characterisation ("how much does the reference constrain?")
For each KPI on Batch 3:
- distribution plot with the grey-pore and cracked sub-populations coloured;
- robust centre/spread (median, MAD) next to classical (mean, SD), computed **with and without the known-anomalous sites**, both shown;
- **minimum detectable change (MDC)** at the configured α and 80 % power for an incoming batch of n sites (n = 7 by default), obtained by simulation: draw n-site pseudo-batches from the full usable reference **without replacement** (a with-replacement draw rejected 11 % at zero shift — D23), add a shift, compare against the remaining reference sites with the §2.4 test, and find the shift detected 80 % of the time. n is the batch's usable n for that KPI (e.g. 5 for Batch 1 bright-phase KPIs). Computed for the five primary KPIs (headline) and the secondary KPIs (appendix). Measured on this data (HL statistic): crack_frac 1.75, pore_max_d 1.75, pore_frac 2.0, bright_frac 1.5, bright_d50 2.5 reference MADs. The MDC is printed next to every "consistent" result;
- **reference-split diagnostics** (internal, with uncertainty — not a verified production false-alarm rate): repeated random splits of the reference into n-vs-rest, run through the full §2.4 + §2.7 pipeline, with every reference-dependent fit repeated inside each split. Outcomes are counted **separately**: statistical drift alerts (the only ones comparable to α), local-anomaly flags and credible-anomaly calls, and quality-related abstentions (an *investigate* caused by image quality is an abstention, not a false positive). The split (7 vs 10) is smaller than the real comparison (7 vs 17), so the drift-alert rate is reported as a diagnostic with its own interval, not as validation of the 7-vs-17 procedure. Reference-vs-identical-reference is kept only as a software sanity check.

### 2.4 Pairwise comparison engine (handcrafted KPIs)
For every ordered pair (1 vs 3, 2 vs 3, 1 vs 2, unseen vs each):
- per KPI: robust standardised shift (difference of medians / reference MAD) with an approximate bootstrap-over-sites 95 % interval, Cliff's delta, and a **site-level permutation p-value on the Hodges–Lehmann shift** (exact enumeration at 7 v 17; the difference of medians has only ~56 attainable values at these n and 21 % of its null p-values equal 1 — D23); Holm correction across the **five primary KPIs only**; secondary KPIs reported uncorrected and labelled descriptive;
- multivariate: energy distance on the standardised primary KPIs (consequence-weighted, §2.6b) with a site-label permutation p-value; a second, descriptive energy distance on all trusted KPIs;
- per-site drift score (distance of each site to the reference centre) to show whether a shift is batch-wide or carried by a few sites;
- **local-anomaly check** (separate path): per-site maxima of consequential KPIs (crack-like void fraction, largest void, additive agglomerate size, intra-particle crack density) and the extreme 512-px patches, compared with the per-site maxima **of the ordinary reference sites**; exceedance of the ordinary-reference maximum raises an evidence flag; the flag is promoted to a credible anomaly only under the severity, reliability and image-review conditions of §2.0 rule 5, with the margin reported; the cracked reference sites are plotted on the same axis as known anomalies, never folded into the "normal" range.

### 2.5 ML corroboration (two members, never the sole driver)
With seven independent sites per incoming batch, several models would mostly agree on shared confounders. We start with one, and keep one exploratory catch-all because it is the only component that can see what the KPIs do not encode.

| Member | Signal | Explanation | Calibration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grouped classifier two-sample test** — L1-logistic regression reference-vs-batch on **material KPIs only** (boundary sharpness and other flags excluded — D24; a flag-inclusive run is shown separately as acquisition evidence); patches always stay with their site (GroupKFold), sites carry equal weight, and preprocessing is re-fitted inside every fold and permutation; cross-validated AUC against a **Monte Carlo** null from site-level label permutations (count stated). With 24 sites this is a corroborator and a KPI-ranker, not a detector; the null band for AUC will be wide and is shown | is the batch separable at all, and by which KPIs | coefficients (and SHAP) per KPI with direction | site-level permutation null |
| **Patch-embedding novelty (exploratory)** — DINOv2 ViT-S/14 features of per-image-normalised 512-px BSE patches, PCA fitted on the reference (re-fitted per LOO fold), kNN distance. On this data it tracks acquisition (bse_std, curtaining, black level) more than any KPI and its ranking is resolution-dependent (D25) | may catch structure the KPIs do not encode; here mostly acquisition | *where*: novelty map on the strip; nearest reference patches; correlation of novelty with each KPI and with each acquisition flag (reported so a reader sees what it is tracking) | reference leave-one-site-out novelty distribution, PCA re-fitted in each fold |

Deferred: Gaussian-mixture patch density and isolation forest (the latter's default fits each tree on all reference observations without bootstrap, so there is no out-of-bag score to calibrate with). They can be added if the two members above prove insufficient on the unseen batch.

Use in the decision: a classifier that separates (AUC beyond the site-permutation null) corroborates a KPI drift and names the KPIs; a novelty signal that fires when no trusted KPI moves forces *investigate* with the novelty map as evidence. Agreement between members is reported but is not treated as independent confirmation.

### 2.6 Sensitivity to acquisition adjustment
Regression adjustment cannot establish that a share of the shift *was caused by* the microscope — boundary sharpness and bright-phase separation can change with the material itself. Three views are shown for each comparison:
1. **unadjusted** (§2.4 as is);
2. **stratified**: the same comparison restricted to sites with matching acquisition flags (e.g. excluding low-contrast and grey-pore sites);
3. **adjusted sensitivity**: KPIs residualised on the acquisition variables (bright-phase separation, black level, stretch fraction, curtaining, boundary sharpness), comparison re-run.
The report states how much the shift attenuates under 2 and 3, as a sensitivity analysis, with the three-channel agreement score alongside. No causal percentage is claimed.

### 2.6b Physics layer (conditional, qualitative)
The layer turns geometric KPIs into statements a materials engineer can act on, and supplies sanity checks. After review it makes **qualitative, relative** statements only; no performance percentages. D36 and `docs/battery_microstructure_review.md` distinguish observed geometry from application hypotheses. Fresh graphite–Si/SiOx material is confirmed; conditional mechanism claims still require validation. The production wording corrections were applied in D37/E25, with regenerated known-batch reports; the literature review distinguishes observed geometry from unvalidated battery mechanisms.

| Block | From KPIs | What we state | What we do **not** claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stereology** (secondary until assumptions are checked) | area fractions; 2-D additive diameters | conditional volume fractions requiring representative section sampling and phase labels; 3-D additive size distribution by Schwartz–Saltykov unfolding (additive only, assumes near-spherical particles); nominal mass fraction conditional on actual Si / SiOₓ density and binder assignment, to compare with the recipe | exact Si versus SiOx chemistry; representative volume sampling or valid unfolding assumptions from material-family confirmation alone |
| **Macro-pore structure** | pore fraction, pore size, through-image-depth profile | direction of the measured geometry change **only when it exceeds the ±5-level threshold band**; which image-depth bin has the lowest segmented void fraction; the single fact that no site has a 2-D top-to-bottom macro-pore path (`fraction_connected = 0`, D25); wetting/transport/packing questions requiring further evidence | a monotonic transport ranking from 2-D pore area or equivalent diameter; effective conductivity values; that 2-D tortuosity bounds 3-D; anything about unresolved porosity |
| **Void geometry** | crack-like voids, largest void, void orientation | how many sites contain voids long enough to interrupt a through-thickness column of solid in 2-D, void length relative to coating thickness, delamination index | electronic continuity (a 2-D connection is a geometric observation) |
| **Additive size and rate** | observed 2-D size quantiles (primary) and unfolded quantiles (secondary) | matched-quantile section-size comparison (D10/D50/D90 each vs the reference's own quantile); size may alter local kinetic/mechanical susceptibility, with chemistry, contact and electrolyte access unresolved | an observed lithiation-time ratio from squared section diameters; equal 2-D sampling bias between batches; absolute diffusion times |
| **Additive mechanics** | observed phase amount, section size, ridge appearance and reviewed local neighbourhood | conditional local-mechanics questions for later formation/cycling; phase amount and particle scale treated separately; ETD ridge-pixel coverage with interpretation pending | greater total expansion/binder load from size alone; intact-particle share from 1 minus ridge coverage; electronic contact from residual-solid adjacency; stress values |
| **Consequence weights** | — | an ordinal weight per KPI (high: void geometry, additive loading/size, macro-porosity and gradient; medium: pore shape, texture scale; low: flags, confounded KPIs), documented and used by §2.4's multivariate test and §2.7 | — |

Dropped: areal-capacity proxy (frame height cannot carry it while cropping and orientation are unconfirmed).

**Physics sanity checks on our own measurements** (reported, not hidden):
- Phase fractions sum to 1; threshold-sensitivity band (±5 gray levels) shown for every fraction.
- Measured open-pore fraction (6–14 %) is far below typical calendered-anode porosity (25–35 %). This is *consistent with* unresolved porosity inside the binder / carbon-black network being counted as solid, but it does not prove that explanation: segmentation thresholds and sample preparation (e.g. resin or smearing filling fine pores) remain alternatives. Stated explicitly with the alternatives; a question for the organisers; a reason to consider a binder-network class and to run the threshold-sensitivity band.
- Pore elongation and void alignment describe void sections. ETD dominant ridge orientation is a curtaining diagnostic. Graphite plate alignment needs reviewed graphite instances and known collector direction before a transport-anisotropy hypothesis.

Caveats attached to every physics statement: 2-D sections, nominal 25 nm/px, fresh uncycled graphite–Si/SiOx confirmed by Santosh; exact chemistry, recipe, binder, collector orientation and pixelwise labels unspecified. No cycling/performance outcomes. Existing voids or ridges are not interpreted as cycling damage, SEI or lithium plating.

### 2.7 Decision
Two parallel checks, then one verdict.

**Check A — batch-wide drift** (primary KPIs only). (i) Multivariate shift beyond the matched permutation null? (ii) Carried by primary KPIs rather than by flags, secondary or confounded KPIs? (iii) Consistent across sites (share of sites beyond the ordinary-reference range)? (iv) Does the classifier corroborate?

**Check B — localized defect.** Evidence flags: any site whose per-site maximum of a primary KPI exceeds the ordinary-reference maximum, or any extreme patch flagged by §2.4's local check or §2.5's novelty map (expected on ~40 % of clean 7-site batches for a single KPI, so flags alone mean little). Credible anomaly: a flag that meets the severity margin, measurement-reliability and image-review conditions of §2.0 rule 5. Severity and prevalence are reported for both.

| Verdict | Meaning | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Consistent with working reference** (within detectable limits) | no evidence of drift or localized defect at the sensitivity this reference allows; MDC listed per KPI | A negative on all four, B negative |
| **Investigate — batch-wide drift** | the batch differs; drivers named | A(i) positive but (ii), (iii) or (iv) not all met, or the shift attenuates strongly under §2.6 stratification/adjustment, or only confounded KPIs / novelty moved, or n too small |
| **Investigate — localized anomaly** | one or more sites carry a credible anomaly | B credible (not merely flagged), regardless of A |
| **Reject (provisional)** | drift is large, physically consequential, consistent, corroborated, and robust to acquisition adjustment | A(i)–(iv) all positive and the §2.6 views agree, or B credible on several sites with large severity |

"Accept" as a release decision requires an equivalence test against practical tolerances on the high-consequence KPIs; tolerances do not exist yet, so the notebook reports *consistent* and flags release as provisional. Provisional tolerances derived from the physics layer are proposed for discussion with the organisers.

**Decision stability** (not "probability the verdict is correct"): the share of leave-one-site-out re-runs returning the same verdict (seven re-verdicts for a 7-site batch), with the approximate bootstrap share as a secondary number. Each verdict lists the single change that would move it to the adjacent category. All thresholds used here are the frozen ones from §2.1, under the provenance statement of §2.0 rule 7. Verdict outcomes are always reported in three separate columns — drift alert, localized anomaly, quality abstention — so that abstentions are never counted as alarms.

### 2.8 Explanation layer
- Driver sentences generated from the numbers, followed by a scoped battery hypothesis: for example, a measured reduction in segmented void fraction prompts a packing/wetting review, with threshold sensitivity and site uncertainty shown; it does not establish worse ionic transport. Examples are format illustrations, not observed results.
- Evidence images: crack-like voids painted, additive particles outlined, novelty map, most-typical and least-typical site side by side, the extreme patch behind any localized-anomaly flag.
- KPI table: reference range (with and without known-anomalous sites), batch value, shift, interval, permutation p, MDC, trust level, consequence weight, flag.
- Acquisition flag table and the three §2.6 views.
- A "limits of this dataset" paragraph (§2.0 rule 9) on every report.

### 2.9 Self-test, false alarms and power
- **Reference-split diagnostics** (§2.3): drift-alert rate with interval next to α; local flags, credible anomalies and quality abstentions tallied separately; stated as internal diagnostics, not production false-alarm rates.
- **Power / MDC** per KPI by simulation (§2.3), and for the multivariate test.
- **Synthetic-shift injection** on reference features (and on images for the embedding member): scale one KPI by a known factor and show at what effect size each component fires.
- **Heterogeneity check**: ordinary reference sites vs the full reference should return *investigate — localized anomaly* (the cracked sites), demonstrating that known anomalies are not absorbed.
- Reference vs identical reference: software sanity check only.

## 5. Risks and mitigations

- **Reference heterogeneity swamps the null** → with/without known-anomalous sites shown; MDC per KPI; localized path keeps anomalies visible.
- **n = 7 sites** → §2.0 in full: five primary KPIs, exact permutation, jackknife stability, MDC on the first screen, no tuning on the known batches, patches never counted as n.
- **Embedding tracks acquisition** → per-image normalisation; novelty correlation with flags reported; exploratory label; cannot drive a verdict alone.
- **Pretrained weights unavailable at the venue** → network confirmed reachable now; fallback is a torchvision model cached in advance or a Gabor/LBP texture bank.
- **Time** → working verdict exists after 2.3 + 2.4 + 2.7; ML, physics and HTML polish are additive.
